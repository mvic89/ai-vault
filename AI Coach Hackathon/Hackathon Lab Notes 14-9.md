And they were thinking out loud and, like, talking about what they were doing.  And.  And it was really interesting to see that the key difference that he could notice among the successful problem solvers and the less successful was their willingness to rethink, to go back and take a different approach.  So, yeah, like they said, the novices, they picked an idea and they tried to ride that idea all the way to the end, where the people that were much more successful and can actually solve the problems faster were those that took a few ideas, spiked them out, tried, and then were willing to go back and then go in the right direction.

And there's something called the Education Endowment foundation that published a big, or they conducted a big survey of the available research in this area that they published, and they came up with this simple approach where you first plan, you understand the goal, you monitor how it's going when you're working, and then you evaluate.  And the interesting thing here is that the monitoring and evaluation should not be done after you've run into problems.  They should be done before you run into problems.  And, like, you should constantly kind of think about, okay, am I heading in the right direction?  Like, is there a better way to rethink this?
